# Discovery Report

**Product:** Workshop Queue (synthetic example)
**Version / date:** 1.0 / 2026-11-26
**Status:** DRAFT — SYNTHETIC, NOT MARKET-VALIDATED
**Geography:** Not selected
**Prepared from:** Fictional product brief only; no interviews or external research were conducted

## 1. Executive assessment

- Problem and target buyer: Hypothetical repair-job visibility for owners of independent vehicle repair workshops
- Primary user(s): Service advisors and workshop managers
- Proposed value: A shared view of active jobs, status and promised collection dates
- Current confidence: Low; this is a scenario for testing factory workflow, not evidence of demand
- Recommendation: Gather real workflow and buyer evidence before deciding whether to proceed to product-specific architecture

## 2. Buyer and user

- Economic buyer: Workshop owner (hypothesis)
- Daily user: Service advisor and workshop manager (hypothesis)
- Purchase trigger: Missed customer commitments or difficulty coordinating active jobs (hypothesis)
- Existing workaround: Paper job cards, spreadsheets and messaging apps (hypothesis)
- Who controls budget: Workshop owner (hypothesis)

No buyer or user interviews have been conducted.

## 3. Problem and workflows

The scenario assumes that repair-job details and promised collection dates can be scattered across paper and messaging tools. The proposed workflow is vehicle check-in, job creation, status updates, manager review and job completion. Frequency, urgency, job volume and the cost of missed commitments are unknown and require direct observation.

## 4. Alternatives and competition

| Alternative / competitor | Evidence and source date | Relevant capability | Gap or differentiation hypothesis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paper job cards | Synthetic scenario assumption; not researched | Familiar manual job tracking | Shared visibility may be limited; unverified |
| Spreadsheets | Synthetic scenario assumption; not researched | Flexible job lists | Updates may be inconsistent; unverified |
| General messaging apps | Synthetic scenario assumption; not researched | Team communication | Structured job lifecycle may be absent; unverified |

No competitor research was performed. These are workflow alternatives in the fictional scenario, not verified market findings.

## 5. Market and geography

The initial market is intentionally unspecified. The owner must select a country and city before evaluating competitors, legal requirements, language, currency, timezone, data residency, sales channels or market size. No market-size estimate is claimed.

## 6. Business model hypotheses

- Likely payer and pricing unit: Workshop owner; monthly subscription per workshop
- Pricing hypothesis and evidence: Subscription is a hypothesis only; no willingness-to-pay evidence exists
- Acquisition and sales motion: Direct demonstrations to workshop owners may be testable; no channel is validated
- Retention / recurring-value hypothesis: Daily job tracking may create repeat use; this remains untested
- Main commercial risks: Low willingness to pay, entrenched manual workflows, onboarding burden and existing software alternatives

## 7. MVP boundary

### Included

- One workshop organization
- Owner and staff membership
- Customer and vehicle references
- Repair job creation and status changes
- Promised collection date
- Basic open, delayed and completed job views

### Excluded

- Payments and accounting
- Parts inventory
- Customer notifications
- AI features
- Multi-branch analytics
- External integrations

### Deferred

- Automated reminders
- Advanced reports
- Integrations with accounting or parts systems

## 8. Risks and unknowns

| Risk / unknown | Impact | Evidence gap | Validation action | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Workshops may not experience this as a high-priority problem | High | No interviews or observations | Observe real job intake and status workflow | Product owner |

## 9. Recommendation and next decision

Do not treat this example as market validation. The next decision for a real product would be whether to select a target geography and conduct a small set of documented workshop interviews. Only evidence from that work could support a decision to prepare a real architecture package. This report does not authorize implementation.
